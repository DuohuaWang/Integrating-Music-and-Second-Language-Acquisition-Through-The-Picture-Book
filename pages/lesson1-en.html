Class Plan

Lesson 1 · The Very Hungry Caterpillar

Basic information

Teaching object
Suitable for children who are complete beginners in learning music and English, or have experience in just one field.
Class plan designer
王鐸樺 (Duo hua Wang)
Class time
60 mins
Teaching material
Story book - A very hungry caterpillar, visual/teaching aid card
Student learning conditions
Auditory Discrimination: The basic ability to hear the difference between short and long sounds.
Vocal Mimicry: Students simply need the ability to listen and repeat sounds after the teacher.

Aims

To develop students’ natural English speaking rhythm and intonation using musical beats and The Very Hungry Caterpillar.

Cognitive: Vocabulary Recognition, Syllable and beats Concepts

Psychomotor: Rhythmic Manipulation, Spoken Pronunciation

Affective: Stimulating learning motivation, Cultivating language sense and musicality

Concrete aims

Cognitive

1-1
Vocabulary Recognition – Able to recognize fruits on picture cards and know their corresponding English words
1-2
Syllable and beats Concepts – Understand that different words have different lengths or “beats.”

Psychomotor

2-1
Rhythmic Manipulation – Able to transform spoken syllables into physical movements or instrumental actions.
2-2
Spoken Pronunciation – Able to pronounce the taught English words correctly.

Affective

3-1
Stimulating learning motivation – When children gain a sense of accomplishment, making them more willing to speak English or participate in musical activities.
3-2
Cultivating language sense and musicality – Allowing the body to naturally feel the rhythm of English pronunciation and the pulse of musical rhythm.

Activity details

一、Preparation Activity5 min

Motivation: The teacher shows oversized picture cards with Vocab.
Warm up Activity: Demonstrates the picture cards can disassembly and assembly.
Tell students the objective of this lesson: these food words with the correct syllables and rhythm.

Concrete aims
1-1 · 3-1
Resources
Picture cards (oversized), word cards
Evaluate
Students can name the fruits on the picture cards and show interest in the lesson.

二、Develop Activity — Activity 1: Syllable Tap-Tap10 min

The teacher reads the target words aloud and guides students to tap the rhythm using instruments (e.g., tambourines, maracas) or just tap on the desk.

Concrete aims
1-2 · 2-1 · 2-2
Resources
Tambourines, maracas (or the desk)
Evaluate
Taps match the number of syllables; students repeat the word correctly.

Activity 2: Guided Practice10 min

Students take turns practicing at their desks. Upon hearing a syllable, students follow the syllable to drop the corresponding number of blocks into their own small box, ensuring they successfully link the “heard rhythm” with a “physical quantity.”

Concrete aims
1-2 · 2-1 · 3-1
Resources
Blocks, small boxes
Evaluate
The number of blocks dropped matches the syllables heard.

Activity 3: Word & Rhythm Match10 min

The teacher introduces the written target words using specially designed word & Rhythm cards. As shown in the visual aid, each card displays the word split into syllables (e.g., “O-ran-ge”) with corresponding musical notes printed right above them. The teacher guides students to touch each musical note while tapping their instruments, seamlessly linking the visual text, the musical rhythm, and the number of syllables.

Concrete aims
1-1 · 1-2 · 2-1 · 2-2
Resources
Rhythm cards, tambourines, maracas
Evaluate
Students touch each note while tapping and say the word with the correct syllables.

三、Comprehensive Activities — Activity 3: Word & Rhythm Match10 min

The teacher introduces the written target words using word cards. To help students connect the text with the syllables, each word card has a music note. Students are guided to place their blocks directly onto the dots under the written words, successfully linking the visual text, the spoken rhythm, and the physical quantity.

Concrete aims
1-2 · 3-1 · 3-2
Resources
Word cards with music notes, blocks
Evaluate
Blocks are placed on the dots under the word correctly; students show a sense of accomplishment.
